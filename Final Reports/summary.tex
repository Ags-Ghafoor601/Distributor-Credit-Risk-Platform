\projecthead{01\;\; The Ledger — Distributor Credit Risk Engine}
{skillSYNC Problem 01}
{A credit scorecard that reads a distributor's own payment ledger and returns a defensible 300--900 risk score for every dealer.}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{2.75cm}X@{}}
\lbl{Product} &
Web application (FastAPI + Next.js) that ingests three raw CSV exports --- dealers, salesmen, invoice history --- and returns a ranked risk table, per-dealer reason codes, and a downloadable Word risk memo for each account. Deployed and publicly accessible. \\[1pt]

\lbl{Target audience} &
Owners of Pakistani FMCG, pharmaceutical and textile distribution houses managing 50--500 dealers, together with the finance leads and 3--10 field salesmen operating under them. Deliberately built for businesses with \emph{no} ERP and no data team. \\[1pt]

\lbl{Problem} &
Trade credit is extended on a salesman's word --- \emph{``yaar, acha banda hai''} --- with no structured assessment. Distributors report 3--7\% of revenue lost to bad debt, PKR~1M+ in annual write-offs, and 100+ hours a year chasing payments. The salesman who vouches carries none of the downside risk. \\[1pt]

\lbl{How it works} &
Six locally-grounded behavioural signals are engineered from raw invoice history: post-dated cheque bounce rate, Eid/Ramzan-adjusted lateness, salesman-vouch bias (leave-one-out), territory clustering, PKR-inflation-adjusted exposure, and order-frequency trend. A Weight-of-Evidence logistic scorecard converts these to a 300--900 score with plain-language reason codes. Features and outcomes are split temporally, so the model predicts future behaviour rather than restating the past. \\
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=parch,colframe=rule,boxrule=0.4pt,arc=1.5pt,
                  left=6pt,right=6pt,top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
{\color{navy}\bfseries\footnotesize ADVANTAGE OVER ALTERNATIVES}\\[2pt]
\footnotesize
\textbf{vs.\ salesman judgement:} surfaces risk in accounts the sales team personally vouches for --- five such dealers were flagged in the reference portfolio.\;
\textbf{vs.\ credit bureaus:} works where no bureau coverage exists for small retailers.\;
\textbf{vs.\ black-box ML:} every score decomposes into named, auditable reasons an owner can challenge.\;
\textbf{vs.\ generic scorecards:} adapts to each uploaded portfolio --- reads unfamiliar column names and date formats, derives its own feature window and market-specific late-payment threshold, and trains a portfolio-specific model when the general one is a poor fit.\;
\textbf{Uniquely, it reports its own reliability} and declines to score rather than producing confident output on unsuitable data.\\[3pt]
{\color{slate}\scriptsize \textbf{Limitation:} validated on constructed portfolios with known outcomes; no real-world default has been observed yet. The RED/AMBER/GREEN tier is the reliable signal, the exact score directional.}
\end{tcolorbox}

\projecthead{02\;\; Law Firm RAG System — Legal Research Assistant}
{skillSYNC Problem 07}
{A retrieval-augmented assistant that answers questions from a firm's own case files and drafts legal memos with traceable citations.}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{2.75cm}X@{}}
\lbl{Product} &
A Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline over a law firm's private document corpus, served through a React interface. Supports natural-language querying of case files and automated generation of structured legal memos grounded in retrieved source passages. \\[1pt]

\lbl{Target audience} &
Small and mid-sized Pakistani law firms --- partners, associates and paralegals --- who hold years of case history in unindexed folders of PDFs and scanned documents. \\[1pt]

\lbl{Problem} &
Legal research inside a firm's own archive is manual, slow and dependent on whoever remembers the file. Junior associates spend hours locating precedent that already exists in-house, and knowledge leaves the firm when a lawyer does. \\[1pt]

\lbl{How it works} &
Documents are chunked and embedded using multilingual embeddings suited to English/Urdu legal text, then indexed in a ChromaDB vector store. A router query engine directs each question to the appropriate retrieval strategy, and the generation layer answers strictly from retrieved context --- with citations back to the source document, so no assertion is unverifiable. \\
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=parch,colframe=rule,boxrule=0.4pt,arc=1.5pt,
                  left=6pt,right=6pt,top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
{\color{navy}\bfseries\footnotesize ADVANTAGE OVER ALTERNATIVES}\\[2pt]
\footnotesize
\textbf{vs.\ manual search:} reduces precedent-finding from hours to seconds across the firm's entire archive.\;
\textbf{vs.\ general-purpose chatbots:} answers only from the firm's own documents, with citations --- eliminating the fabricated-case-law risk that has already drawn sanctions in foreign courts.\;
\textbf{vs.\ foreign legal platforms:} handles Pakistani legal material and mixed English/Urdu text, which international products do not cover.\;
\textbf{vs.\ cloud legal SaaS:} the corpus stays under the firm's control, preserving client confidentiality and privilege.\\[3pt]
{\color{slate}\scriptsize \textbf{Limitation:} answer quality is bounded by the quality and coverage of the firm's own archive; the system retrieves and cites, it does not exercise legal judgement.}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{6pt}
{\color{slate}\scriptsize
Both projects target Pakistani small and mid-sized businesses underserved by international software: sectors where the data is real but unstructured, and where an explainable, locally-grounded system is worth more than a technically sophisticated opaque one.}
